01AI product · Stanford capstone · 2026

Halo

Your work, represented.

I designed an AI layer that turns the work people already do, from meetings and commits to design edits and tickets, into status updates in their own voice, sized for a teammate, a manager or an executive. It speaks for you, never about you.

View case study
Halo · Home
Trust mode CuratedBalancedAmbient Routine updates share themselves. Sensitive ones wait for you.

Take status updates off people’s plates without turning their work into something to be monitored.

02Mobile · Syracuse capstone · 2025–26

Arriba Perú

Safer work. Clearer rights. In the phone they already have.

I led the research and design of a bilingual mobile platform, co-designed with 15 domestic workers in Lima: verified families, pay checked against the legal minimum, rights read aloud, Yape and cash in one wallet, and help that never draws attention.

View case study
  • Verified familiesAnonymous ratings from past workers.
  • Fair-pay checkEvery offer against Peru’s legal minimum.
  • Rights, read aloudSix color-coded doors replace the maze.

Live prototype. Tap the screen to use it.

  • Every sol, accounted forYape syncs, cash takes one tap.
  • Learn by videoA listen-only mode for busy hands.
  • Help, quietlySoft check-ins and one slide to alert.

“Safety in domestic work today is not built into the system. It is built into relationships.”

Insight from my conversation with Dr. María Amalia Pesantes, medical anthropologist
Goal
Bring safety, clarity and opportunity to Peru’s 420,000 to 500,000 domestic workers, most of whom work behind closed doors.
Challenge
Design it with them, not for them, from Syracuse, on the tools they already trust: WhatsApp video calls and voice-note diaries.
Result
A working Android-first prototype in Spanish and English, with six flows from sign-up to a discreet emergency alert.
Role
Sole designer and researcher
Co-designers

Guidance
Dr. María Amalia Pesantes, medical anthropologist

03Concept · iOS · 2025

AI Native iOS

AI, under your thumb.

My concept for an AI-native iPhone. The assistant moves into the dock, answers land on the home screen, and nothing sends, books or changes without your yes. I designed it for iOS 26, then rebuilt it when iOS 27 shipped.

View case study
  1. 1
    Answers land on your home screen.Small questions get small answers. Your apps stay put.
  2. 2
    It shows you before it acts.A preview, one clear button and five seconds to undo.
  3. 3
    You can see where it thinks.On iPhone, Private Cloud, or the assistant you chose.

Live prototype. Tap the screen to use it.

  1. 4
    Only one dock slot moves.Phone, Messages and Mail never do, so muscle memory works.
  2. 5
    Your history is one swipe up.Not in a separate app. Kept on your iPhone for 30 days.
  3. See how each one traces back to the research
Goal
Make AI feel like part of iOS, not another app you have to open.
Challenge
iOS 27 made the assistant smarter, but typing to it still starts at the top of the screen, and its history lives in a separate app.
Result
A working iOS 27 prototype with nine flows, from quick answers to a booked table, and a clear record of how it grew out of version 1.
Role
Sole designer, both versions
Type

How I work

Not a straight line.

Six moves, in whatever order the problem needs. I lead every one. AI follows, pinging me with patterns, options and critiques I check against real people. I work with the latest models, Claude first and ChatGPT second, and switch the day a better one ships.

My attack: find the real question, build it for real, try to break it, ship the whole package.

Go where people already are.

I pick research methods that fit the people: WhatsApp video and voice-note diaries in Lima, 45-minute interviews across three roles for Halo, five sources for iOS.

Claude summarizes and sorts what I collect, so patterns show up sooner.

I choose who to ask, how to ask, and what counts as evidence.

Find the real question.

I sort every note by theme until the pattern turns into one question a team can design against.

Claude and ChatGPT widen my options for wording and framing.

I decide which question is true to the evidence.

Make it real, early.

Every project on this site is a working product: a nine-flow iOS prototype, an Android-first app, a full web app with live AI.

AI-assisted code, mostly with Claude, turns my Figma designs into working builds, fast.

I own the layout, the interaction, the motion and every state.

Attack my own work before anyone else does.

Ten hard objections for Halo, sketches back to participants in Lima, a keep-or-fix critique of my own version 1, contrast checked on every pairing.

Claude helps me generate and critique early concepts.

Real people’s reactions decide what survives.

Decide with receipts.

Every decision traces back to a research note or a critique, so a team can follow the reasoning, and challenge it.

AI never makes the call.

Layout, interaction and trust are my judgment.

Hand over the whole package.

Engineers get working code, design tokens and guidelines, not just mockups. Founders get decks and films. My handovers are written for someone who never met me.

Claude is built into how I produce deliverables: scripts render charts, documents and film frames.

I set the system they follow, and check every file.

Every project here went back at least once, and the decision that defined it came after.

About

Behind the work.

I think in systems and human factors, not just screens.

I trained in Industrial and Interaction Design at Syracuse, studied UX Design for AI Products at Stanford, and I’m now earning a Master’s in Communication Design at Parsons in New York.

I move fast and collaborate closely. I ship with engineers, run research with users, and present with confidence to product, marketing and leadership. I’ve owned design end to end, from a startup’s first logo directions to a product carried through its Series A, so I know how to own scope and translate ambiguity into direction.

I’m curious by default. I test new AI models the day they ship and keep a running list of design details from the products I use, always looking for the newest approach that genuinely serves people. I design for the brand in front of me, not the trend of the week, and I work fluently in English and Spanish.
