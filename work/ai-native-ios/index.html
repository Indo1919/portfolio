AI, under your thumb.

A concept for iOS 27. The assistant moves into the dock, answers land on your home screen, and nothing sends, books or changes without your yes.

Chapter 1

What I was seeing.

I started with iOS itself: how the home screen has changed, what each era added, and what people were saying about iOS 26 right now.

How the home screen got here

Every era changed how you get to things. None of them changed where you ask for things.

  1. 2007iPhone OS 1

    A grid of apps and a dock. The model we still use.

  2. 2010iOS 4

    Folders, the first way to organize the grid.

  3. 2013iOS 7

    Flat and layered. Depth comes from blur and translucency.

  4. 2020iOS 14

    Widgets on the home screen, and the App Library.

  5. 2024iOS 18

    Icons anywhere, tinted icons, Apple Intelligence.

  6. 2025iOS 26

    Liquid Glass. Controls react to what's behind them.

  7. 2026iOS 27

    Siri AI, asked from the Dynamic Island at the top.

  8. This conceptThe dock asks

    Ask from the place your thumb already rests.

Step 3: Turn the pattern into a question

What I saw

Design and ecosystem were praised wherever they came up. AI split people: excited about the features, frustrated by lag and inconsistency.

What I concluded

The gap wasn't visual quality. AI lived in separate apps and features, so using it meant leaving what you were doing.

What I asked

How might AI feel like part of the home screen, instead of another app to open?

Chapter 3

Then iOS 27 shipped.

In September 2026, Apple released iOS 27 with Siri AI. It holds real conversations, understands what's on screen, and keeps past chats in a new Siri app. Typed questions start from the Dynamic Island at the top of the screen.

That answered half of my original question: the iPhone now has a capable assistant built in. It left the other half open. Where should it sit, and what should it be allowed to do for you? So I went back to version 1 and marked it up.

Chapter 5

Five decisions.

Each one traces back to something from the research or the critique of version 1.

Answers land on your home screen.

Small questions get small answers. A card rises from the dock and sits over your apps, which stay exactly where they were. Tap outside it and it's gone.

Traces back toKeep #3, and the research: people praised polish and consistency everywhere, so a new layer shouldn't move anything.

It shows you before it acts.

Reading is instant. Anything that sends, books or changes a setting shows a preview with one clear button. After you tap it, it waits five seconds before it really happens, so Undo actually works.

iOS 27 keeps past conversations in a separate Siri app. Here they're one swipe up from the dock, kept on your iPhone for 30 days unless you change it.

Where AI helped, and where it didn't.

In version 1, I used ChatGPT during research to summarize how iOS had changed and where the market was heading, and AI helped me generate and critique early concepts.

Every layout, interaction and component was designed by me in Figma, and the renders were made in Illustrator and Photoshop. For the 2026 revamp, I rewrote the concept around iOS 27 and rebuilt the prototype in HTML with AI-assisted code.

What I'd test next.

Does one moving slot help, or just surprise people?A week-long diary study would show whether people start relying on it or stop looking at it.

Does anyone read the chip?If nobody taps it, trust has to come from somewhere else, like how each card is worded.

Is a preview too slow for close friends?Texting a partner might deserve a faster path than booking a table. Testing would show where that line sits.
