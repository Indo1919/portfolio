01 The problem

How do you explain a product nobody can see?

Scowtt works in the background. It reads a company's lead data, scores every lead and follows up with prospects on the company's behalf. "AI SDR" was a brand-new term, and a sales leader had to understand it, and trust it, in one scroll.

It's invisible

The work happens inside a CRM and an inbox, not on a screen anyone watches.

It's new

AI SDR was a new category, so the name alone explained nothing.

It asks for trust

A business is letting software talk to its future customers.

It kept moving

An early product with an early pitch. The design had to hold up as the story changed.

02 The approach

Explain it in the order it works.

Every lead takes the same path: first it's qualified, then an AI agent follows up. I built the whole story around those two steps and carried them through the brand, the website and the product.

04 The original website

A homepage that answers four questions, in order.

The original site took a busy sales leader from "what is this?" to "let's talk" without making them work for it.

  1. What is it?Five words over the lattice: AI SDR that drives outcomes.
  2. How does it work?Two rows in the order the product works: qualification, then sales development.
  3. Why trust it?The mission names the problem before any product is shown.
  4. What next?One taupe button, Get started, and a short sign-up form.

06 The outcome

Where it landed.

What I handed over, what happened next, and what I'd do differently now.

What I handed over

  • Brand research and logo directionsThe attributes the brand had to carry, and four directions to build from.
  • The color paletteEight colors with clear jobs, from the page itself to the one action color.
  • The original websiteBuilt around clarity, conversion and trust for B2B buyers.
  • A UI system and app screensA Figma framework for the web and mobile app, refined with product and engineering.

What happened next

  • The Series AScowtt raised a $12M Series A in December 2025. I was its designer through that stretch.
  • A design team took it furtherAs the company grew, a design team made the current logo and website.

What I'd do differently

  • Check contrast on every pairingWhite on my taupe button measures 3.3 to 1, under the 4.5 to 1 guideline. A black label keeps the color and fixes it.
  • Design with real content soonerMy wireframes used grey bars where words would go, which made reviews abstract.
  • Show the reason behind every scoreThe first version had no place to see why a lead was ranked. The rebuild adds that.
